Operational concept (OV-1) · draft for discussion · 2026-11-11

Many people explore a problem. A team of agents builds the answer.

go-build-it is an application that builds applications. Stakeholders with different, sometimes conflicting, aims work out what is needed while AI team members turn each agreed slice into working, evidenced software. What people learn from the software changes what they ask for next.

The ideas behind the picture

01

Agreement is the scarce thing

Agents make building cheap and fast. What stays slow is people working out what they want and settling where they disagree. go-build-it spends stakeholder attention carefully.

02

Discovery never finishes

Requirements and mockups are expected to change. The system is built for a moving target: every change shows what it touches.

03

Conflict is information

When two aims pull apart, that is recorded with who holds each side and how it was settled. The reasons become the project's values.

04

People decide, agents do

Agents interview, draft, build and review. People choose between options, approve baselines and approve releases.

05

Small true slices

Only a small, agreed slice is built at a time, end to end, test first. Working software arrives early and tests the understanding.

06

Evidence, not assurance

Each release carries the tests that ran, the screens against their mockups, and the record of who approved what.

Where it stands

In go-build-it today

  • The team: six roles of AI members, each in its own harness, worktree and terminal pane.
  • The documents: requirements, mockups, plan and tracker with stable IDs, linked requirement to mockup to task to screen.
  • The human loop: questions with recommendations, option sets, review gates, dress rehearsals and recorded values.
  • The rules: global standards in every agent's instructions, and a separate reviewer for every change.
  • The record: every action with who, what, when and why. The same operations in the UI, the CLI and MCP.

What the vision adds

  • Stakeholders as first-class: named people or perspectives, each with objectives and a say at gates.
  • A conflict record: which aims disagree, who holds them, how each was resolved.
  • Change impact: a changed requirement marks the mockups, tasks and code that rest on the old one.
  • Baselines: a named, approved set of requirements and mockups that a slice is built against.
  • Scale: a large system split into areas, each with its own glossary, owners and rules.
  • Evidence packages: the record assembled for whoever must authorise the system.
go-build-it vision. A draft to argue with: the picture should change as the requirements do.
