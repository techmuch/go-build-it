Decided Nov 20 (Q13, ADR-016): Take 1, the admin console, is the design. Section D adds what comes first: dev mode with a user switcher, and a personal Assistant for every user. Takes 2 and 3 are kept as a record; Take 3's sign-off routes and access review return in a later phase on top of Take 1. Requirements rev 13, FR-190–FR-206.

What this is. Requirements rev 12 add users, sign-in and per-project access control (§4.4, FR-160–FR-189). These are three takes on how it could look in Approach A. The two screens every take needs, signing in and the sign-in settings, are drawn once at the top. Each take then shows four screens. Pick one, or mix parts of them.

Decisions already made (Nov 12): one shared go-build-it server for everyone · local accounts first, OIDC required next (provider not chosen) · in multi-user mode loopback is not trusted unless a setting says so, and the setting names the user it signs in as · custom roles from the start, as flexible as custom artifact tabs.

One idea runs through all three: a team role is the job someone does on a project (PM, developer, QA…), filled by a person or an AI. An access role is what they're allowed to do (view, comment, approve, release…). Maria can be the project's reviewer and hold Approver access. AI members get access roles too, and can never hold more than the person who directs them.

Look for: can you tell, at a glance, who can approve the release of ledgerly, and why? Could you set up a new kind of project (say, a regulated one) without asking a developer?

Take 2

Project-first: access lives with the team

Access is managed where the work is. A project's Team page lists people and AI members together, each with a team role and an access role. Project admins invite people straight into their project. Custom roles are defined per project in docs/gbi.toml, just like custom artifact tabs: versioned, reviewed and committed by Courier. They are capped by the server's limits, so a repository file can never grant more than the server allows. Admins keep a thin People list for statuses and sign-in.

The ledgerly roles are its own, read from docs/gbi.toml. Invite to ledgerly sends an invitation that grants access here only. Pending access requests come from people who opened a link they can't see yet.

Take 3

Stakeholders & sign-off policy

Access follows the software factory's stakeholders and the stage of the work. You manage stakeholder groups (Product owners, Cybersecurity, DevSecOps, UI/UX, Transformation, Sponsors, Developers) and a policy: what each group may do in each stage, Discover → Design → Build → Verify → Release. Projects pick a policy template ("Internal tool", "Regulated"). Gates route sign-off to the right groups automatically, like Jira workflows. Custom roles are the cells of the matrix.

3a

Policy templates: who may do what, at each stage

FR-171FR-174FR-179FR-182

Look for: one matrix per template. Rows are stakeholder groups, columns are stages, and each cell is a level: A admin · S signs off · C contributes · R reviews and comments · V views · – none. Clicking a cell opens the exact permissions behind it, so each level is a custom role. "Regulated" adds a second sign-off and takes Build access away from sponsors. AI members are a row too, capped at C.

D · comes first

Dev mode and the personal Assistant

Dev mode lets one person on one machine play two users and try multi-user work before sign-in exists (FR-190–FR-193). Every user has a personal Assistant, always running while they're signed in, shown in the top bar, and the one you talk to in every side chat (FR-194–FR-199). Harnesses are paid for by one shared subscription or by each user's own credentials, and are kept warm so replies start quickly (FR-200–FR-206).

D1

The dev bar: switch users in one click

FR-190FR-191FR-192FR-193

Look for: the bar is a colour nothing else uses, on every screen. You switch to maria and the whole UI becomes hers: her inbox, her projects, her Assistant. The right side says why your last request was allowed or denied, so access rules are checked as you click.
